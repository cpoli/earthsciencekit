Glaciology examples
===================

One executed example per breakthrough in :doc:`/history/glaciology_breakthroughs`,
in chronological order, each with an asserted check of the result it shows.
Download the Python source or notebook from each page. :doc:`/guides/glaciology` records units and
limits; all examples use deterministic synthetic inputs and require no downloads.

A Glen glacier therefore shears mostly near its bed and moves almost as a block above it.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Johannes Weertman studied the junction between an ice sheet grounded below sea level and its floating shelf. The ice at the grounding line has the flotation thickness, set by the water depth, and the shelf spreads at a rate that rises as a power of that thickness. A grounding line on a bed that deepens inland therefore cannot be steady: a small retreat thickens the ice at the junction, which discharges more ice, which drives further retreat. The example compares the snowfall feeding the ice sheet with a measure of the discharge, the spreading of 10 km of cold shelf times the flotation thickness.">

Because ice sheets are thin, the flux at each point follows from the local thickness and surface slope, and the thickness obeys a nonlinear diffusion equation solved on a staggered grid. Its steady state on a flat bed is Vialov&#x27;s (1958) profile, flatter at the dome than Nye&#x27;s parabola. simulate_flowline_glacier applies the same scheme to a mountain glacier, here grown on a bare bed and then shown responding to a 100 m rise of its equilibrium line.">

Climate examples
================

One executed example per breakthrough in :doc:`/history/climate_breakthroughs`,
in chronological order, each with an asserted check of the result it shows.
Download the Python source or notebook from each page. :doc:`/guides/climate` records units and
limits; all examples use deterministic synthetic inputs and require no downloads.

A planet in radiative balance emits what it absorbs, which for the present albedo of about 0.3 gives an effective temperature near 255 K. The measured mean surface temperature of about 288 K is 33 K warmer; that gap is the greenhouse effect.">

She suggested that more of it in the air would warm the Earth. The simplest model of that effect is one atmospheric layer, transparent to sunlight, that absorbs a fraction of the infrared from the ground and re-emits it both up and down. A fully absorbing layer warms the surface by a factor of 20.25.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Roger Revelle and Hans Suess showed that seawater resists taking up carbon dioxide. Because of carbonate chemistry, a 10% rise in the CO2 pressure of the air is balanced by only about a 1% rise in the dissolved carbon of the surface ocean. This ratio, about 10, is the buffer (Revelle) factor. In equilibrium the share of added carbon left in the air is 1 / (1 + M_o / (R M_a)) for atmospheric and oceanic inventories M_a and M_o, so the ocean holds far less of it than a simple partition by inventory would suggest. Inventories here are round illustrative values.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Syukuro Manabe and Richard Wetherald computed the temperature profile of a single atmospheric column in which radiation and convection balance. Where radiative equilibrium alone would be unstable, convection holds the temperature at a fixed lapse rate. With relative humidity held fixed, doubling CO2 warmed the surface by about 2.3 K. In this simplest version, the troposphere passes through the effective temperature at the emission height, and more CO2 lifts that height. The surface then warms by the lapse rate times the lift. The model keeps the stratosphere at a fixed skin temperature, so it misses the stratospheric cooling that Manabe and Wetherald also found.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Mikhail Budyko and William Sellers independently built energy-balance models in which ice reflects more sunlight than ocean or land. Colder climates grow more ice and absorb less, which cools them further. Both found that a drop of a few percent in sunlight could freeze the whole planet, and that a frozen Earth would stay frozen until the Sun grew far brighter: a hysteresis loop. Inverting the energy balance gives the solar constant that holds each temperature, which traces every equilibrium at once.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Klaus Hasselmann showed that slow climate variability needs no slow cause. The ocean integrates fast, random weather forcing, just as a heavy particle integrates molecular kicks in Brownian motion. The result is red noise: a spectrum flat below the frequency lambda / (2 pi C) and falling as frequency squared above it. Here a seeded white-noise forcing drives a 50 m mixed layer, sampled every month for 4000 years.">

It concluded that doubling CO2 would warm the Earth by 3 K, probably between 1.5 and 4.5 K. It also warned that ocean heat capacity would delay the warming by decades. In a one-box model the delay is the time scale C / lambda, so a more sensitive climate (a smaller lambda) also responds more slowly.">

CO2 forcing is logarithmic, 5.35 ln(C / C0), because its strongest bands are saturated. Methane and nitrous oxide grow with the square root of concentration, less a term for the band near 7.7 μm that they share. The IPCC adopted the formulas in 2001; from 1750 to 1998 they give about 1.46, 0.48 and 0.15 W/m² for the three gases.">

Each year the net top-of-atmosphere imbalance N = F - lambda T is plotted against the warming. The intercept is the forcing, the slope is minus the feedback, and the line crosses zero at the equilibrium warming, without running to equilibrium. Here the run is a one-box model with seeded annual noise added to the imbalance.">

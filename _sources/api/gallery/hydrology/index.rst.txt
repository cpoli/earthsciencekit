Hydrology examples
==================

One executed example per breakthrough in :doc:`/history/hydrology_breakthroughs`,
in chronological order, each with an asserted check of the result it shows.
Download the Python source or notebook from each page. :doc:`/guides/hydrology` records units and
limits; all examples use deterministic synthetic inputs and require no downloads.

The river carried only about a sixth of the rain that fell on its basin, so rainfall was more than enough to feed it, against the old view that rivers rise from the sea through the ground.     <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Antoine de Chézy was asked to size a canal bringing the water of the Yvette to Paris. He reasoned that in uniform flow the weight of the water down the slope balances a bed friction that grows as the square of the velocity, so the mean velocity is C sqrt(R S), with R the cross-sectional area over the wetted perimeter. The example evaluates a rectangular canal: deepening it raises the hydraulic radius and the velocity, which tends to the wide-channel limit where R equals the depth.">

Evaporation stops when the air is saturated and turns into condensation when the air holds more vapour than the surface.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Henry Darcy built sand filters for the fountains of Dijon and measured the flow through a vertical column of sand under different heads. The discharge was proportional to the head lost across the column divided by its length, with a constant that depends on the sand, now the hydraulic conductivity. The example repeats the experiment: heads read on the manometers combine elevation and pressure, every sand gives a straight line through the origin, and water in the pores moves faster than the flux by the inverse porosity.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Jules Dupuit assumed that groundwater beneath a gently sloping water table moves horizontally with a velocity uniform on each vertical, driven by the slope of the water table. Darcy&#x27;s law then gives a discharge proportional to the thickness times the slope, which is constant between two rivers, so the square of the water-table height varies linearly and the profile is a parabola. The example draws the profile and checks that every section carries the same discharge.">

The mean velocity then becomes R(2/3) S(1/2) / n, with one roughness number per kind of channel. The example shows that Chézy&#x27;s C is not a constant, and that Manning&#x27;s formula is Chézy&#x27;s with C = R(1/6) / n.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="W. Heber Green and G. A. Ampt pictured water entering a soil as a sharp wetting front, saturated above and dry below, pulled down by gravity and by the capillary suction at the front. The infiltration rate starts high, falls as the wetted zone deepens and tends to the saturated conductivity. Under steady rain the soil first absorbs everything; once its capacity falls to the rain rate the surface ponds and the excess runs off, as in Mein and Larson&#x27;s (1973) treatment, which simulate_infiltration integrates.">

In steady flow the flux is the same at every height, and for Gardner&#x27;s exponential conductivity the suction profile above a water table has a closed form: hydrostatic without flow, a unit-gradient tail under infiltration, and a limit height above which evaporation cannot be supplied from below.">

The example takes a Nash (1957) cascade of reservoirs as the unit hydrograph of a 50 km^2 catchment, convolves it with a three-hour storm, and checks that the flood carries the volume of the excess rain.">

The capacity falls exponentially during a storm from an initial to a steady value. Overland flow produced this way, Hortonian or infiltration-excess runoff, is the shaded area where the rain exceeds the capacity.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Gerald McCarthy, working on flood control in the Muskingum basin of Ohio, wrote the storage of a river reach as a weighted mix of its inflow and outflow, S = K (X I + (1 - X) O). Combined with continuity, the outflow becomes a fixed linear combination of the latest inflows and the previous outflow. Routing a flood through a reach delays it by about K and lowers its peak, more so when X is small, while conserving its volume.">

Two ratios, the bifurcation ratio and the length ratio, summarize the whole network. The example fits both to a synthetic fifth-order basin (with Strahler&#x27;s 1952 ordering) and compares the counts with Horton&#x27;s law.">

The drawdown gained per tenfold increase in time gives the transmissivity, and the time at which the line crosses zero drawdown gives the storativity, without matching a type curve. The example recovers both from a synthetic pumping test.">

Evaporation becomes a weighted mean of a radiation term and a drying-power term that need only the net radiation and standard measurements at screen height. The example solves the full energy balance for the surface temperature and shows that Penman&#x27;s equation agrees to within a few per cent.">

Penman&#x27;s equation becomes the Penman-Monteith equation, which reduces to Penman&#x27;s for a wet surface and falls as the stomata close. The example computes the aerodynamic and surface resistances of the FAO reference grass and the evaporation it gives, a few millimetres a day.">

Their topographic index ln(a / tan(beta)) is high where a large area drains through a gentle slope, at the foot of hillslopes and in hollows. One catchment-mean deficit then sets the local deficit everywhere, the saturated area and an exponentially receding baseflow.">

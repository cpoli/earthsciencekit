Oceanography examples
=====================

One executed example per breakthrough in :doc:`/history/oceanography_breakthroughs`,
in chronological order, each with an asserted check of the result it shows.
Download the Python source or notebook from each page. :doc:`/guides/oceanography` records units and
limits; all examples use deterministic synthetic inputs and require no downloads.

Long waves in that ocean travel at sqrt(g h). To keep pace with the Moon&#x27;s bulge at the equator the ocean would have to be about 22 km deep; the real ocean, about 4 km deep, cannot follow it, so real tides are a forced dynamic response, not an equilibrium.">

    <div class="sphx-glr-thumbcontainer" tooltip="Franz Josef von Gerstner found the first exact nonlinear water wave. Each particle moves on a circle whose radius shrinks as exp(-k b) with its depth b, and the free surface is a trochoid with sharp crests and flat troughs. Because the crests are narrow, the mean surface lies k a2 / 2 below the level of the orbit centres. The wave travels at the deep-water speed sqrt(g / k).">

The dispersion relation omega2 = g k tanh(k h) makes long waves travel faster than short ones. In deep water the energy travels at the group speed, half the speed of the crests, which is why the swell from a distant storm arrives longest waves first.">

Light water stands higher than dense water, so the sea surface above a warm station rises relative to a cold one until the pressure at a deep level of no motion is the same under both. The surface slope then drives a geostrophic current.">

Cold water sinking near Antarctica must upwell through the abyss, and the upwelling w and downward mixing kappa balance when h = kappa / w. With w about 1.2 cm per day the profile needs a diffusivity of about 1e-4 m^2/s, far more than measured in the interior, a puzzle that took 30 years to resolve.">

earthsciencekit.geophysics.block_magnetic_anomaly(x, left, right, top, bottom, magnetization)[source]#

Magnetic anomaly of a vertically magnetized two-dimensional block.

Strips of normally and reversely magnetized crust give the alternating anomalies over mid-ocean ridges.

Parameters:

  • x (float or array_like) – m; constraint: real. Horizontal position of the observer, perpendicular to the strike.

  • left (float or array_like) – m; constraint: left < right. Edges of the block.

  • right (float or array_like) – m; constraint: left < right. Edges of the block.

  • top (float or array_like) – m; constraint: 0 < top < bottom. Depths below the observation level.

  • bottom (float or array_like) – m; constraint: 0 < top < bottom. Depths below the observation level.

  • magnetization (float or array_like) – A/m; constraint: real. Positive downward (normal polarity at the north magnetic pole).

Returns:

Model value [T]: vertical field anomaly, equal to the total-field anomaly reduced to the pole.

Return type:

numpy scalar or ndarray

Notes

Infinite strike length, uniform vertical magnetization and a vertical ambient field. A laterally infinite layer has no external field. Equation: MU0 * magnetization / (2 * np.pi) * (phi(top) - phi(bottom)) with phi(z) = np.arctan((right - x) / z) - np.arctan((left - x) / z). Invalid, empty, nonreal or nonfinite inputs raise ValueError. NumPy broadcasting applies; singular and overflowing outputs raise ValueError.

Integrate Rikitake’s coupled two-disc dynamo.

  • mu (float) – Positive scalar scaled resistance [1].

  • k (float) – Positive scalar setting the torque asymmetry A = mu * (k**2 - 1 / k**2) [1].

  • time_scale (float) – Positive scalar seconds per nondimensional time unit [s].

Gravitational constant from a torsion balance.

The twist of a suspended rod between large masses, timed by its free swing, measures G.

numpy scalar or ndarray

Notes

Point masses, a massless rod and attraction by the near large ball only; the small mass cancels. Equation: 2 * np.pi**2 * arm_length * separation**2 * deflection / (attracting_mass * period**2). earthsciencekit.geophysics.virtual_geomagnetic_pole(site_latitude, site_longitude, declination, inclination)[source]#

Virtual geomagnetic pole of a site direction.

Poles from rocks of one age on one continent should agree; when they wander, the continent moved.

Parameters:

  • site_latitude (float or array_like) – rad; constraint: -pi/2 <= latitude <= pi/2.

  • site_longitude (float or array_like) – rad; constraint: real.

  • declination (float or array_like) – rad; constraint: real.

  • inclination (float or array_like) – rad; constraint: -pi/2 <= inclination <= pi/2.

Returns:

Model value [rad]: pole latitude and pole longitude in [0, 2 pi).

Return type:

ndarray, shape (…, 2)

Notes

The pole of the geocentric dipole that gives the observed direction at the site; a reversed direction gives a southern pole. Its angular distance from the site is p = np.arctan2(2 * np.cos(I), np.sin(I)), along the great circle leaving the site at azimuth D. Equation: pole = site * np.cos(p) + (north * np.cos(D) + east * np.sin(D)) * np.sin(p) with the site’s unit position vector and local north and east unit vectors; latitude np.arctan2(z, np.hypot(x, y)). Invalid, empty, nonreal or nonfinite inputs raise ValueError. NumPy broadcasting applies; singular and overflowing outputs raise ValueError.

earthsciencekit.geophysics.zonal_field(latitude, distance, degree, coefficient, reference_radius)[source]#

Field of one zonal Gauss coefficient.

Expanding the potential in spherical harmonics separates the dipole from smaller, faster-decaying parts.

Parameters:

  • latitude (float or array_like) – rad; constraint: -pi/2 <= latitude <= pi/2. Geocentric latitude.

  • distance (float or array_like) – m; constraint: positive. Distance from the Earth’s centre.

  • degree (float or array_like) – 1; constraint: integer >= 1.

  • coefficient (float or array_like) – T; constraint: real. Gauss coefficient g_n^0; for zonal terms Schmidt and unnormalized Legendre functions agree.

  • reference_radius (float or array_like) – m; constraint: positive. Usually 6371.2 km.

Returns:

Model value [T]: the northward and the downward component.

Return type:

ndarray, shape (…, 2)

Notes

Internal sources only, potential V = reference_radius * sum((a / r) ** (n + 1) g_n^0 P_n(sin latitude)). Sum several calls for a truncated zonal model; non-zonal terms (m > 0) are not included. Equation: (a / r) ** (n + 2) * coefficient * [lpmv(1, n, x), -(n + 1) * lpmv(0, n, x)] with x = np.sin(latitude), a = reference_radius, r = distance and n = degree. Invalid, empty, nonreal or nonfinite inputs raise ValueError. NumPy broadcasting applies; singular and overflowing outputs raise ValueError.

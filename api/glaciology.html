earthsciencekit.glaciology.dansgaard_delta(temperature, slope, offset)[source]#

Oxygen-isotope ratio of precipitation from the air temperature.

Air loses its heavy isotopes first as it cools and rains out, so the snow at colder sites is lighter.

Parameters:

  • temperature (float or array_like) – K, mean annual surface air temperature; constraint: positive.

  • slope (float or array_like) – permil/K, about 0.69; constraint: positive.

  • offset (float or array_like) – permil, the delta at 0 degC, about -13.6; constraint: real.

Returns:

Model value [permil], delta-18O relative to SMOW. Scalar input returns a NumPy scalar; arrays broadcast.

Return type:

numpy scalar or ndarray

Notes

Dansgaard (1964): a spatial relation for mid- and high-latitude coastal and polar stations; the temporal slope at one site can differ. Equation: slope * (temperature - 273.15) + offset. Invalid, empty, nonreal or nonfinite inputs raise ValueError. NumPy broadcasting applies; singular and overflowing outputs raise ValueError.

earthsciencekit.glaciology.dansgaard_johnsen_age(depth, thickness, kink_height, accumulation)[source]#

Age of ice at depth in the Dansgaard-Johnsen flow model.

The first time scale for a deep ice core, Camp Century, integrated the thinning of annual layers down to the kink and below it.

Parameters:

  • depth (float or array_like) – m of ice below the surface; constraint: between 0 and thickness, exclusive of thickness.

  • thickness (float or array_like) – m of ice; constraint: positive.

  • kink_height (float or array_like) – m above the bed; constraint: between 0 (exclusive) and thickness.

  • accumulation (float or array_like) – m/s of ice; constraint: positive.

Returns:

Model value [s]. Scalar input returns a NumPy scalar; arrays broadcast.

Return type:

numpy scalar or ndarray

Notes

Dansgaard and Johnsen (1969), the integral of the reciprocal of dansgaard_johnsen_downward_velocity; it tends to nye_age as the kink height goes to zero. Below, z = thickness - depth, h = kink_height, a = accumulation and s = 2 * thickness - h. Equation: s / (2 * a) * np.log(s / (2 * np.maximum(z, h) - h)) + h * s / a * (1 / np.minimum(z, h) - 1 / h). Invalid, empty, nonreal or nonfinite inputs raise ValueError. NumPy broadcasting applies; singular and overflowing outputs raise ValueError.

earthsciencekit.glaciology.dansgaard_johnsen_downward_velocity(depth, thickness, kink_height, accumulation)[source]#

Vertical ice velocity in the Dansgaard-Johnsen flow model.

Ice frozen to its bed shears only near the bottom, so the vertical velocity falls linearly down to a kink and then quadratically to zero at the bed.

Parameters:

  • depth (float or array_like) – m of ice below the surface; constraint: between 0 and thickness.

  • thickness (float or array_like) – m of ice; constraint: positive.

  • kink_height (float or array_like) – m above the bed; constraint: between 0 (exclusive) and thickness.

  • accumulation (float or array_like) – m/s of ice; constraint: positive.

Returns:

Model value [m/s], downward; a year’s layer is this speed times one year thick. Scalar input returns a NumPy scalar; arrays broadcast.

Return type:

numpy scalar or ndarray

Notes

Dansgaard and Johnsen (1969): steady state, constant horizontal strain rate above the kink and a linear decrease below it, no basal melting. Below, z = thickness - depth and h = kink_height. Equation: np.where(z >= h, accumulation * (2 * z - h) / (2 * thickness - h), accumulation * z**2 / (h * (2 * thickness - h))). Invalid, empty, nonreal or nonfinite inputs raise ValueError. NumPy broadcasting applies; singular and overflowing outputs raise ValueError.

Age difference between the ice and the air trapped in it.

Air stays connected to the atmosphere through the porous firn until the bubbles close, so the gas in a core is younger than the ice around it.

Parameters:

  • close_off_depth (float or array_like) – m, depth of bubble close-off; constraint: nonnegative.

  • ice_density (float or array_like) – kg/m^3; constraint: positive.

  • accumulation (float or array_like) – m/s of ice; constraint: positive.

Returns:

Model value [s]; about 4 kyr at present-day Vostok. Scalar input returns a NumPy scalar; arrays broadcast.

Return type:

numpy scalar or ndarray

Notes

Schwander and Stauffer (1984), as used for Vostok by Barnola et al. (1987): steady firn densification with no thinning above close-off; diffusion through the open pores, which makes the gas slightly older, is neglected. NumPy broadcasting applies; singular and overflowing outputs raise ValueError.

earthsciencekit.glaciology.glen_surface_velocity(thickness, surface_slope, rate_factor, exponent, density, gravity)[source]#

Surface velocity of a glacier deforming by Glen’s law.

The surface speed rises with the fourth power of thickness and the cube of slope, which is why thick glaciers flow so much faster than thin ones.

Parameters:

  • thickness (float or array_like) – m; constraint: positive.

  • surface_slope (float or array_like) – 1, tangent of the surface slope; constraint: nonnegative.

  • rate_factor (float or array_like) – Pa^-n/s; constraint: positive.

  • exponent (float or array_like) – 1, Glen’s n; constraint: at least 1.

  • density (float or array_like) – kg/m^3; constraint: positive.

  • gravity (float or array_like) – m/s^2; constraint: positive.

Returns:

Model value [m/s]. Scalar input returns a NumPy scalar; arrays broadcast.

Return type:

numpy scalar or ndarray

Notes

Nye (1952) laminar flow with Glen (1955) rheology, no sliding. Equation: 2 * rate_factor / (exponent + 1) * (density * gravity * surface_slope) ** exponent * thickness ** (exponent + 1). Invalid, empty, nonreal or nonfinite inputs raise ValueError. NumPy broadcasting applies; singular and overflowing outputs raise ValueError.

Return type:

TimeSeries

Notes

Solves dH/dt = b(b_bed + H) - dq/dx with the shallow-ice flux q = -D ds/dx of shallow_ice_diffusivity() and the balance of linear_mass_balance(), by the method of lines on a staggered grid in Mahaffy’s (1976) form, with integrators.integrate_ode (BDF) in time. The ends carry no flux, the thickness is kept nonnegative (ablation tapers to zero over the last metre of ice, and a solver overshoot below zero relaxes back over about a year, so the right-hand side stays continuous), and there is no sliding, calving, width variation or isostasy.
